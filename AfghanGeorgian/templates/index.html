About Julian Day Numbers

Julian Day Number (JDN)

The Julian Day Number is a continuous count of days since the beginning of the Julian Period on January 1, 4713 BCE in the proleptic Julian calendar. It is widely used in astronomy and historical chronology.

  • Starts from January 1, 4713 BCE
  • Continuous day count with no gaps
  • Used for astronomical calculations
  • Simplifies date arithmetic across calendars
Modified Julian Day (MJD)

The Modified Julian Day is defined as MJD = JD - 2400000.5. It was introduced to provide a more convenient reference point for modern dates and is commonly used in satellite tracking and other applications.

  • Epoch: November 17, 1858
  • Smaller numbers for modern dates
  • Used in space and satellite applications
  • Starts at midnight UTC

About Calendar Systems

Learn about the different calendar systems supported by this converter

Afghan/Persian Calendar

A solar calendar used in Afghanistan and Iran, with 12 months totaling 365 or 366 days. The first six months have 31 days, the next five have 30 days, and the last month has 29 or 30 days in leap years.

  • Type: Solar Calendar
  • Epoch: March 22, 622 CE
  • Leap Cycle: 33-year cycle
  • New Year: Nowruz (Spring Equinox)

Gregorian Calendar

The most widely used calendar system in the world, introduced by Pope Gregory XIII in 1582. It refined the Julian calendar to better align with the solar year.

  • Type: Solar Calendar
  • Epoch: January 1, 1 CE
  • Leap Rule: Every 4 years, except century years not divisible by 400
  • Average Year: 365.2425 days

Islamic Calendar

A lunar calendar used in Islamic countries for religious purposes. Each month begins with the new moon, resulting in a year of approximately 354 days.

  • Type: Lunar Calendar
  • Epoch: July 16, 622 CE (Hijra)
  • Leap Years: 11 years in every 30-year cycle
  • Year Length: 354 or 355 days

Hebrew Calendar

A lunisolar calendar used in Jewish communities for religious observances. It attempts to synchronize lunar months with the solar year through a complex leap year system.

  • Type: Lunisolar Calendar
  • Epoch: October 7, 3761 BCE
  • Leap Years: 7 years in every 19-year cycle
  • Months: 12 or 13 months per year

Julian Calendar

Introduced by Julius Caesar in 46 BCE, this was the predecessor to the Gregorian calendar. It has a simpler leap year rule but accumulated errors over time.

  • Type: Solar Calendar
  • Epoch: January 1, 1 CE
  • Leap Rule: Every 4 years without exception
  • Average Year: 365.25 days

Kurdish Calendar

Based on the Persian calendar but with Kurdish month names. It follows the same structure and leap year rules as the Afghan/Persian calendar system.

  • Type: Solar Calendar
  • Epoch: March 22, 622 CE
  • Structure: Same as Persian calendar
  • Language: Kurdish month names
